*Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562.

## Facts

Mrs Donoghue's friend bought her a bottle of ginger beer at a café in Paisley, Scotland. The bottle was opaque. She said that after drinking some, the remains of a decomposed snail came out of it, and that she suffered shock and gastroenteritis. She had no contract with the café or the manufacturer, so she sued the manufacturer, Stevenson, in negligence.

## Issues

Does a manufacturer owe a duty of care to the ultimate consumer of its product, with whom it has no contract?

## Held

Yes, by a majority of three to two (Lords Atkin, Thankerton and Macmillan; Lords Buckmaster and Tomlin dissenting). The case could go to trial on the facts alleged.

## Ratio

A manufacturer of products sold in a form showing that they are meant to reach the ultimate consumer as they left the manufacturer, with no reasonable possibility of intermediate examination, owes the consumer a duty to take reasonable care in preparing them.

## Obiter

Lord Atkin's **neighbour principle**: 'You must take reasonable care to avoid acts or omissions which you can reasonably foresee would be likely to injure your neighbour.'[^1] Your neighbours are 'persons who are so closely and directly affected by my act that I ought reasonably to have them in contemplation'.[^2]
