A promise is enforceable as a contract only if it is supported by consideration: something given or promised in return for it, as the price of it.

## Elements

1. **Bargain**: the promisor asks for the consideration as the price of the promise, and it is given in return. A gift on a condition is not a bargain.[^1]
2. **Need not be adequate**, but must be of some value in the eyes of the law.
3. **Must not be past**: something done before the promise, and not at the promisor's request, is not consideration.

## Issues to look for

- Performance of an existing duty.
- A promise to accept part payment of a debt.
- Promises that are illusory.

Compare the structure of the torts notes in [[Duty of care]].

[^1]: *Australian Woollen Mills Pty Ltd v Commonwealth* (1954) 92 CLR 424; *Beaton v McDivitt* (1987) 13 NSWLR 162.
