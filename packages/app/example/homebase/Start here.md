This is an example homebase: a few weeks of notes for a law subject, to try Quire on. It is an ordinary folder of Markdown files, so change anything, or delete it when you are done. Your own homebases are not touched.

## Find your way

- **The Docket** is the way into everything. Press its key (Super+D: ⌘D on a Mac, Ctrl+P on Windows, unless you changed it under Settings → Hotkeys) and type: note names, words inside notes, commands. `?` in the Docket lists every shortcut.
- **Subjects** (in the Docket) shows the subjects and topics: here, [[Duty of care|Torts → Negligence]] and [[Consideration|Contracts → Formation]]. Subjects are the top folders; topics are the folders inside them.
- **Switch homebase** (Ctrl+Shift+O, or the homebase name at the bottom left) goes back to your own notes.

## What to try

1. Open [[Duty of care]], a topic summary. Its authorities link to case notes like [[Donoghue v Stevenson]], a case brief: the Docket's **Outline** lists Facts, Issues, Held, Ratio and Obiter and adds a missing one.
2. Open [[Week 3 lecture]]. The recording plays inside the note. Click a timestamp to jump to it, or press **Alt+T** while it plays to add one where you are. Alt+K pauses, Alt+J and Alt+L skip back and forward.
3. Follow a page link in [[Reading notes]]: the reading opens beside the note at that page. Select a sentence in it and press **Alt+Q** to quote it into your note, with a link to the printed page.
4. Work through the [[Exam checklist]]: an issue-by-issue scaffold for answering a negligence problem question.
5. Run **Create example essay** from the Docket to see AGLC4 citations checked as you write.

The recording and the reading are samples made for this homebase: the recording is quiet, with a chime at each timestamp, and the reading is a short explanation, not a real judgment or report.
