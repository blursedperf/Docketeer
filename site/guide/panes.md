# Panes and layouts

Put a case beside your brief, the reading beside your notes, or three things at once, all from the keyboard.

## Splitting

| Keys | |
|---|---|
| **⌘\\** | Split to the right |
| **⌘⇧\\** | Split down |
| **⌘⌥ arrows** | Move to the pane that way |
| **⌘⌥⇧ arrows** | Take this tab to the pane that way (a new pane if there is none) |
| **⌘⌃ arrows** on a Mac, **Ctrl+Alt+[ ] = -** on Linux | Narrower, wider, taller, shorter |
| **⌘⇧=** | Even the panes out |
| **⌘⇧Enter** | Zoom this pane to fill the window, and back |
| **⌘W** / **⌘⇧T** | Close a tab / bring the last one back |
| **Ctrl+Tab** | Next tab in the pane |

From the Docket, **⌘Enter** opens a result to the right and **⌥Enter** below. The same note open in two panes stays
in step as you type, each with its own cursor.

With a mouse, drag a tab along its bar to reorder it, onto another pane to move it (its edges split it), or to an
edge of the window.

## Stacked tabs

**Toggle stacked tabs** (or *Stack tabs* in a tab's menu) lays a pane's tabs side by side at a reading width,
scrolling sideways. Tabs fold into spines with their titles at the edges; click one to bring it back.

## Layouts

The **Layout** panel (**⌘6**, or `>layout`) has ready-made layouts and your own:

- **Single**: one pane with every tab in it.
- **Brief + Source**: your brief on the left, the judgment or reading on the right.
- **Revision**: notes side by side, with the Docket pinned on Authorities.
- **Exam**: your exam outline on the left (wider), scratch notes on the right.

**Save this layout…** keeps the current arrangement under a name. The layout is saved with the homebase and comes back
when you open it.
